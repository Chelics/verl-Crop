Decide whether the image should be adapted to the target aspect ratio by
cropping or by padding.

Choose "crop" only when there is a purposeful crop at the target aspect ratio
that preserves all visually important content with natural margins. Content
that may be removed includes empty space, decorative borders, repetitive
background, edge clutter, and other peripheral material that is not needed to
understand the image or the news story.

Choose "pad" when every reasonable crop at the target aspect ratio would remove
important content, make the composition uncomfortably tight, or make the image
incomplete or harder to understand. Padding preserves the complete source image
and adds background outside it. Important content may include primary subjects,
faces, actions, interactions, essential context, text, labels, legends, axes,
data, map regions, diagram sections, screenshots, and complete item sets.

Use the title only to identify relevant content that is actually visible in the
image. Do not assume that title entities or events are present unless you can
see them.

Do not decide from the image category alone. A photograph may require padding
when its subjects or context cannot be cropped safely. A chart, diagram, map,
poster, or screenshot may still be cropped when only empty margins, decorative
framing, or clearly nonessential peripheral content would be removed.

the important content, choose "crop" only when removing low-value peripheral
content creates a meaningfully cleaner composition; otherwise choose "pad".
