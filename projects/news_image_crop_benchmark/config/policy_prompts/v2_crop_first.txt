News title: {title}
Target aspect ratio (width / height): {target_ratio}

Create a purposeful editorial crop by removing visible content that contributes
the least to the news story. Use the title only to identify relevant content
that is actually visible in the image; do not assume that title entities or
events are present unless you can see them.

First identify the visual content that must survive the crop: the primary
subject, identity-defining features, actions and interactions that explain the
event, and any nearby context needed to understand the story. Treat these as
protected content. A crop is invalid if it makes the protected content unclear,
changes the apparent event, or removes an essential participant or object.

Then look inward from all four image edges for content that can be removed
safely. Prefer to crop away empty space, excessive headroom or foreground,
repetitive or weak background, edge clutter, partial bystanders, unrelated
objects, and other visible regions that do not improve identification, story,
or composition. Incidental text and branding may be removed when they are not
part of the subject or necessary context.

Choose the most compact crop that removes a meaningful amount of low-value
content while preserving all protected content with a natural visual margin.
Do not keep the full image when a clearly cleaner and more focused crop exists.
Do not crop merely to make the subject larger: every removed region must have
lower editorial value than the content retained. When further tightening would
remove useful context or create awkward framing, stop.

For people, never crop through the eyes, nose, mouth, chin, or other
identity-defining facial features. Avoid awkward cuts through the head, neck, or
major joints. Lower bodies, peripheral limbs, background people, and surrounding
space may be removed only when they are not necessary to understand the action,
relationship, or event. Preserve meaningful gestures, held objects, and groups
whose interaction forms the story.

For products, devices, artworks, and other objects, preserve the parts needed to
recognize the object and understand its function. Remove display space,
background props, or peripheral objects when they do not add editorial meaning.
Do not cut through essential controls, logos, screens, or functional features.

For charts, maps, diagrams, posters, screenshots, product labels, and other
informational graphics, treat content-bearing text, labels, legends, axes, data,
and sections as protected content. Crop only decorative borders, empty margins,
or clearly nonessential peripheral material. If a tighter crop would make the
message incomplete or harder to interpret, use the largest crop that fits the
target ratio and preserves the information.

Evaluate each target aspect ratio independently. For a wide target, remove
low-value vertical content before sacrificing useful horizontal relationships.
For a square target, remove the least important side content while keeping the
subject and story coherent. Do not reuse a default center or area from another
ratio.

Return exactly one JSON object with exactly three integer fields:
{"cx_pct": CX_PERCENT, "cy_pct": CY_PERCENT, "area_pct": AREA_PERCENT}

cx_pct is the horizontal center as an integer percentage from 0 to 100.
cy_pct is the vertical center as an integer percentage from 0 to 100.
area_pct is the crop area as an integer percentage from 1 to 100.

The renderer applies the target aspect ratio automatically.
Do not output pixel coordinates, pixel dimensions, pixel area, tags,
Markdown, explanations, or additional fields.
